Astoria currently has two major train lines that run through it:
* The IND Queens Boulevard Line
* The BMT Astoria Line

An improvement that would provide a large benefit to Astorians is the implementation of CBTC on these two lines.

## What is CBTC
Communications-based train control, or CBTC, is a railway signaling system that uses telecommuniciations to provide a more accurate location of the train compared to the automatic block signaling, or ABS, system currently used. The ABS system divides the track into a series of blocks and the signaling system could detect whether blocks are occupied or available and automatically determine whether the train can proceed to the future blocks. CBTC is a continuous system, so it can detect train positions more accurately, reducing headways and delays, providing more accurate arrival time estimates, and increasing safety.

## Prioritizing CBTC
When discussing improvements, it makes sense to consider improving entire lines together. This will then need to be further broken down by station, track lines, rolling stock, etc.

For that reason, the following information involves trains that run on either the IND Queens Boulevard Line or the BMT Astoria Line, even if the train does not run in Astoria (generally).

The IND Queens Boulevard Line runs the:
* E Train
* F Train
* F Express Train
* M Train
* R Train

The BMT Astoria Line runs the:
* N Train
* W Train

Even if we account for the fact that several of the stations on the IND Queens Boulevard Line service other trains, it would still clearly service more people than the BMT Astoria Line. The MTA has analyzed the different train lines and is currently implementing CBTC on the IND Queens Boulevard Line.

CBTC has already been implemented on two lines, the IRT Flushing Line and the BMT Canarsie Line. This is because these are the only two lines that only host a single service and they do not share operating trackage with other lines or services. The new challenge becomes implemented CBTC on a line that carries trains that will also run on ABS lines.

For example, the E train will need to run on ABS on the IND Archer Avenue Line, CBTC on the IND Queens Boulevard Line, and then ABS on the IND Eight Avenue Line again. The current plans will have the IND Queens Boulevard Line fitted with CBTC by 2026.

One phase of implementing CBTC, which has already been completed on the E, F, M, and R trains, but needs to be completed on the N and W trains, is upgrading the rolling stock to rolling stock that supports CBTC. The rolling stock for the N and W trains consist of R46s, R68s, and R68As. None of the R46s, R68s, or R68As can support CBTC. They are planned on being in use until at least 2030, but since there is not currently any plan to implement CBTC on the BMT Astoria line, upgrading the rolling stock is not as much of a priority.

Hopefully, we'll see full CBTC implemented in the coming years, improving the quality of life for all NYC subway users.
